Body Mass Index (BMI) Guidance

Body Mass Index (BMI) is an established clinical metric used to estimate total body fat relative to individual height.


Standard Calculation Formula:

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²


Clinical Reference Ranges:

  • Underweight: < 18.5
  • Normal Weight: 18.5 – 24.9
  • Overweight: 25.0 – 29.9
  • Obesity Class Target: ≥ 30.0

Multi-Condition Disease Risk Analytics Platform

Providing clinical practitioners and individuals with algorithmic probabilistic scoring for early diagnostic evaluation across Diabetes, Hypertension, and Stroke risk profiles.

🩺 What Is This Portal & Who Should Use It?

This portal is an early-stage clinical screening tool designed for general practitioners, healthcare workers, and individual patients who wish to assess their baseline statistical probability for metabolic and cardiovascular conditions. By inputting non-invasive biometric markers—such as blood pressure readings, fasting glucose levels, BMI, and lifestyle habits—the portal generates real-time probabilistic risk scores.

Conditions Evaluated By This Portal:

Hypertension Risk

Evaluates systolic and diastolic arterial pressures combined with age and lifestyle to highlight potential stage 1 or stage 2 essential hypertension.

Diabetes Mellitus

Analyzes fasting blood glucose indicators, body mass index, and physical activity levels to estimate Type-2 diabetes predisposition.

Stroke & Vascular Risk

Integrates blood pressure metrics, heart rate patterns, age, and tobacco usage to compute cerebrovascular event probability.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are disease probabilities calculated? ▼
Calculations utilize Naive Bayes probabilistic models trained on epidemiological health indicators, evaluating systolic/diastolic blood pressure, fasting blood sugar, BMI, and lifestyle metrics.
Who is this portal meant for? ▼
This portal is designed for individuals seeking early preventative assessment and medical practitioners looking for preliminary decision support regarding hypertension, stroke, and diabetes risk factors.
How accurate are the risk percentages shown? ▼
The risk percentages serve as decision-support estimations to highlight elevated risk factors for practitioner review. They should be evaluated alongside standard clinical diagnostic procedures.
